Construction Electrician Salary in Canada 2026

What does a Red Seal 309A Construction Electrician actually earn — from first-year apprentice to industrial journeyperson? Here's the real data.

Updated September 2026

Construction electricians are among the highest-paid tradespeople in Canada, and the shortage of licensed journeypersons has made the labour market even more competitive over the past several years. Whether you're wiring residential projects, commercial high-rises, or industrial facilities, your Red Seal 309A certification is a powerful asset that directly translates into higher wages, better benefits, and negotiating leverage that apprentices simply don't have.

Here's a detailed, current breakdown of what Construction Electricians earn at every stage of their career.

Apprentice Wages: Year by Year

The electrical trade is typically a five-year apprenticeship in most provinces, with wages set as a percentage of journeyperson rates. Union and non-union apprentices can have meaningfully different pay scales, particularly in provinces with strong union density like BC, Alberta, and Ontario.

Union apprentices also receive pension contributions and benefit packages that significantly increase total compensation.

Wages by Province (Journeyperson)

Electrical wages vary significantly by province, driven by union density, local demand (particularly from resource sector projects), and cost of living. On Job Bank's figures (wages updated November 2025), Quebec has the highest provincial median for electricians ($41.30/hr) and Ontario the highest provincial upper figure ($50.50/hr); Alberta's median is $38.00/hr and BC's $34.00/hr.

Who Pays the Most?

Within the 309A trade, specialization and sector matter more than almost any other variable when it comes to maximizing earnings.

Industrial and Shutdown Work

Industrial shutdowns — scheduled maintenance periods at refineries, pulp mills, mines, and chemical plants — consistently pay the highest rates in the electrical trade. Shutdown and turnaround work often adds overtime, daily travel allowances and camp accommodation on top of the hourly rate. For reference, Job Bank's Alberta range for industrial electricians (NOC 72201) is $25.00–$68.00/hr, median $44.00 (wages updated November 2025). The work is intense and may require extended away-from-home periods, but the financial return is unmatched in the trade.

Major Projects and Large General Contractors

Large commercial and industrial projects — data centres, LNG facilities, hospitals, transit infrastructure — offer strong wages and long-term employment stability. On large projects, contractors compete for qualified journeypersons, and pay varies by employer, project and whether the job is covered by a collective agreement.

Government and Institutional Employers

Municipal utilities, school boards, universities, and government facilities hire permanent staff electricians. These positions typically pay mid-range wages but offer exceptional benefits, defined benefit pensions, and exceptional job security. The total compensation picture — especially factoring in pension value — often exceeds private sector roles over a full career.

Residential and Small Commercial Contractors

Smaller residential electrical contractors pay the least in the trade, though the gap has narrowed as the overall labour shortage has pushed wages up across all sectors. These positions can be good for building broad residential skills early in your career but rarely represent the best long-term wage path.

How to Maximize Your 309A Earnings

  • Get your Red Seal as early as possible. The wage jump from 5th-year apprentice to journeyperson is immediate and often significant. Writing the exam before completing your 5th year block release, if eligible, pays off quickly.
  • Pursue industrial certifications. Confined space, H2S Alive, fall protection, and site-specific safety certifications open doors to higher-paying industrial and resource sector jobs that may be otherwise inaccessible.
  • Consider the IBEW. In high-density union provinces, IBEW membership provides access to the Dispatch system, which connects journeypersons with the highest-paying projects available. The hiring hall model can significantly outperform open-market job searching.
  • PLC and automation skills. Electricians who understand programmable logic controllers, variable frequency drives, and industrial automation are in exceptionally high demand at manufacturing and process facilities. These skills can lead to industrial roles; Job Bank's Canada median for industrial electricians (NOC 72201) is $42.00/hr, compared with $35.00/hr for NOC 72200 electricians (wages updated November 2025).
  • Move to major projects. If you're willing to travel or relocate temporarily, major project work in Alberta's oil sands, BC's LNG corridor, or northern resource projects often adds overtime, travel allowances and camp accommodation to the hourly rate; the amount varies by project and agreement.
2026 market note: Canada's energy transition — EV charging infrastructure, battery storage projects, grid modernization — is creating sustained new demand for journeyperson electricians beyond traditional construction cycles. Electricians with experience in renewable energy systems and EV infrastructure are seeing premium offers that didn't exist five years ago.
