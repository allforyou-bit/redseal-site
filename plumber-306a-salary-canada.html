Red Seal Plumber (306A) Salary in Canada 2026 — Province by Province

Residential, commercial, industrial, and fire suppression — plumber wages in Canada vary widely by sector and province. Here is the complete 2026 breakdown.

Updated September 2026

Plumbers in Canada earn a national median of $34.00/hr (Job Bank, 2023–2024 wages) — below gas fitters ($40.00) and construction electricians ($35.00) — and the combination of housing demand, aging infrastructure, mandatory licensing requirements, and an insufficient apprenticeship pipeline has created a chronic skilled plumber shortage that shows no signs of abating. Journeyperson plumbers with UA union membership, gas fitter qualifications, or industrial process piping experience may earn above the national median; Job Bank's national high wage (90th percentile) for plumbers is $46.00/hr, about $92,000 a year at 2,000 hours (2023–2024 wages). This guide breaks down exactly what 306A Plumbers earn in 2026, province by province and industry sector by sector.

Most pay figures on this page are Government of Canada Job Bank data for everyone working in the occupation, not only certified tradespeople. The 306A exam is 125 questions in 4 hours and the pass mark is 70%, which is 88 correct.

Province-by-Province Salary Breakdown

British Columbia — $23–48/hr (Job Bank, 2023–2024)

Job Bank reports BC plumber wages of $23.00 (low), $32.00 (median) and $48.00 (high) per hour for the 2023–2024 reference period, so the BC median sits below the national median of $34.00. These figures include apprentices. Unionized journeypersons are paid under collective agreements negotiated by United Association (UA) locals, whose rates Job Bank does not publish.

Alberta — $20–43/hr (Job Bank, 2023–2024)

Job Bank reports Alberta plumber wages of $20.00 (low), $30.00 (median) and $43.00 (high) per hour for the 2023–2024 reference period, below the national median of $34.00; these figures include apprentices. Union rates for journeypersons are set by United Association (UA) collective agreements, which Job Bank does not publish. Alberta apprentices must be paid at least 50%, 60%, 70% and 80% of the rate of the lowest-paid Plumber journeyperson at the same company in periods 1 to 4 (Alberta Tradesecrets, checked September 2026).

Ontario — $20–50.38/hr (Job Bank, 2023–2024)

Job Bank reports Ontario plumber wages of $20.00 (low), $32.50 (median) and $50.38 (high) per hour for the 2023–2024 reference period; these figures include apprentices. Job Bank does not break out residential, commercial or industrial work, or union rates: unionized journeypersons are paid under collective agreements negotiated by United Association (UA) locals such as Toronto-based UA Local 46.

Saskatchewan — $22–43/hr (Job Bank, 2023–2024)

Job Bank reports Saskatchewan plumber wages of $22.00 (low), $35.00 (median) and $43.00 (high) per hour for the 2023–2024 reference period — a higher median than BC ($32.00), Alberta ($30.00) or the national figure ($34.00). UA Local 179, a United Association local for the piping trades, is based in Regina.

Manitoba — $21–43/hr (Job Bank, 2023–2024)

Job Bank reports Manitoba plumber wages of $21.00 (low), $30.11 (median) and $43.00 (high) per hour for the 2023–2024 reference period, a range that includes apprentices. Manitoba also sets a legal minimum for journeyperson plumbers on industrial, commercial and institutional (ICI) construction projects: $44.13/hr from May 1, 2026 to April 30, 2027, rising to $45.02/hr on May 1, 2027 (Construction Industry Minimum Wage Regulation, checked September 2026). Apprentice plumbers must be paid at least 50%, 60%, 70% and 80% of that journeyperson minimum in levels 1 to 4, or of the journeyperson rate on the same job where no minimum is prescribed (Apprenticeship and Certification — General Regulation).

Industry Sector Wage Comparison

Residential New Construction

Residential plumbers rough in and trim out new homes, townhouses, and low-rise condominiums. This is high-volume work with moderate wages. Job Bank does not break out residential work; its low-to-high wage ranges for all plumbers are $23–$48/hr in BC, $20–$50.38/hr in Ontario and $20–$43/hr in Alberta (2023–2024 wages). The work is physically demanding (rough concrete, tight crawlspaces) but generally provides year-round employment in active markets. New construction residential plumbing is often non-union in Alberta and Ontario, with UA coverage being more prevalent in BC and Quebec for new residential construction.

Commercial and Institutional

Commercial plumbing on office buildings, schools, hospitals, and transit projects is predominantly unionized through UA locals in major centres. The work is more technically complex than residential — larger-diameter pipe, backflow preventer assemblies, medical gas systems in healthcare, grease interceptors in food service — and wages reflect this. UA journeyperson commercial rates are set by each local's collective agreement; Job Bank does not publish union rates, and its national high wage for all plumbers is $46.00/hr (2023–2024 wages). Large healthcare and transit infrastructure projects (hospitals, LRT stations) are particularly well-paying due to project scale and union coverage requirements.

Fire Suppression

Residential and commercial fire suppression system installation is a growing specialty that falls within the plumber's scope in several provinces. In BC, the Code requires residential fire suppression systems in most new single-family homes, creating substantial ongoing demand for plumbers trained in fire suppression. Job Bank places sprinkler system installers in a separate occupational group (NOC 72301, steamfitters, pipefitters and sprinkler system installers), with a national low of $27.00/hr, median of $43.89/hr and high of $55.59/hr (2023–2024 wages). Fire suppression work is also less physically demanding than underground rough-in work, making it an attractive specialty for experienced journeypersons looking for reduced physical wear.

Industrial Process Piping

The highest-paying plumbing work in Canada is industrial process piping — connecting process equipment in manufacturing, chemical, pharmaceutical, and food processing facilities. This work involves specialized materials (sanitary 316L stainless, PVDF, polypropylene, Hastelloy), orbital welding for high-purity systems, strict cleanroom protocols, and detailed inspection and testing requirements. Job Bank does not break out industrial process work; its national low and high wages for all plumbers are $21.00/hr and $46.00/hr (2023–2024 wages). Alberta's petrochemical corridor and Ontario's pharmaceutical manufacturing cluster are the primary employers.

Gas Fitter Premium

One of the most effective ways for a Red Seal Plumber to increase earnings is to obtain a gas fitter license. In every province, gas fitting is a separate licensed activity from plumbing, and holding both allows you to perform a broader scope of work:

  • Alberta Class B Gas Fitter: Covers residential and light commercial gas piping and appliance connections. Most plumbing contractors in Alberta want plumbers who hold this certificate. Alberta's Plumber apprenticeship includes Gasfitter – Class B certification, so apprentices who complete the Plumber program receive both Plumber and Gasfitter – Class B journeyperson certificates (Alberta Tradesecrets, checked September 2026). No official source publishes a separate hourly premium.
  • Alberta Class A Gas Fitter: Covers all gas systems including industrial. High demand in the petrochemical sector. No official source publishes a standard premium; Job Bank's Alberta median wage is $42.27/hr for gas fitters (2024) versus $30.00/hr for plumbers (2023–2024).
  • BC Gas Fitter: BC Gasfitter Class A covers all gas work. In high-demand residential markets (Metro Vancouver, Kelowna), dual-licensed plumbers are significantly more employable. No official source publishes a standard premium; Job Bank's BC median wage is $39.82/hr for gas fitters (2024) versus $32.00/hr for plumbers (2023–2024).
  • Ontario TSSA G2: Covers residential gas appliance installation and servicing. Very common requirement for residential plumbing companies in Ontario. No official source publishes a standard premium; Job Bank's Ontario median wage is $36.06/hr for gas fitters versus $32.50/hr for plumbers (2023–2024 wages).

Overtime, Shift Work, and Self-Employment

Three factors that can significantly increase plumber income beyond base wages:

  • Overtime: Major construction projects and service calls frequently generate overtime at 1.5x the base rate. How much overtime adds to annual earnings depends on the project and season; no official source publishes a typical percentage.
  • Shift work premiums: Industrial plumbers working rotating shifts (days/nights/weekends) may receive shift premiums set by their employer or collective agreement. Oil sands maintenance plumbers on 14/7 rotations earn these premiums in addition to already-high industrial rates.
  • Self-employment: Experienced journeyperson plumbers who establish their own plumbing contracting businesses can earn substantially more than employed plumbers. A busy residential service and renovation plumber in a major city sets their own rates, and net income depends on overhead, volume and how efficiently the business is run.
Plumbing as a business: The plumbing trade lends itself particularly well to self-employment because demand is inelastic — when a pipe bursts or a toilet fails, homeowners call immediately regardless of price. Service plumbers who build a reputation and customer base in a neighbourhood can charge premium rates and earn substantially more than employed journey wages. Red Seal certification is a credibility signal that supports premium pricing in the consumer market.
