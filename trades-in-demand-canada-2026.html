10 Skilled Trades in Demand in Canada 2026 (With Salary Data)

Canada's skilled trades shortage is at a critical point. Here are the 10 trades with the strongest demand, highest wages, and best long-term career outlook — including which ones lead to Red Seal certification.

Updated October 2026 · Wage figures marked Job Bank: 2023–2024 data, updated November 2025

Canada is facing one of the most significant skilled trades shortages in its history. An aging workforce, decades of underinvestment in apprenticeship programs, and massive infrastructure spending have created a genuine labour gap that will only widen through the 2030s. For anyone considering a trades career — or an apprentice wondering if they've chosen the right path — this is genuinely one of the best labour markets in a generation.

The 10 Most In-Demand Trades in Canada

1

Construction Electrician (Red Seal 309A)

Canada's electrification push — EV infrastructure, data centres, and grid upgrades — is driving unprecedented demand for licensed electricians. Job Bank's 3-year outlook for construction electricians (updated December 2025) rates prospects as good in Nova Scotia and PEI, moderate in Ontario, Alberta, Saskatchewan and New Brunswick, and limited in BC, Quebec, Manitoba and Newfoundland and Labrador. Industrial electricians in particular command premium wages in resource provinces.

$20–$48/hr, median $35 (Job Bank, Canada, 2023–2024) Moderate shortage risk to 2033 (ESDC) Red Seal 309A

Heavy Duty Equipment Technician (Red Seal 421A)

Mining, oil sands, infrastructure projects, and construction — every major industry that moves Canada's economy runs on heavy equipment, and those machines need technicians. ESDC projects a strong risk of labour shortage for heavy-duty equipment mechanics nationally over 2024–2033, while Job Bank's 3-year outlook (updated December 2025) rates Alberta prospects as moderate.

Plumber / Steamfitter

Job Bank's Canada median wage is $34.00/hr for plumbers and $43.89/hr for steamfitters and pipefitters (2023–2024 data). Job Bank's 3-year outlook (updated December 2025) rates plumber prospects as moderate in Ontario and limited in BC. Red Seal designations exist for both Plumber and Steamfitter.

Refrigeration & AC Mechanic (Red Seal 313A)

The HFC refrigerant phase-down under the Montreal Protocol, combined with the data centre construction boom, is transforming the 313A trade. ESDC projects a strong risk of labour shortage for refrigeration and air conditioning mechanics nationally over 2024–2033, and Job Bank's 3-year outlook (updated December 2025) rates prospects as good or very good in most provinces but moderate in BC, Quebec, Manitoba and New Brunswick. Mechanics who understand ammonia refrigeration and modern refrigerant alternatives are well placed.

$22–$56/hr, median $37.50 (Job Bank, Canada, 2023–2024) Data centre / industrial Red Seal 313A

Truck & Transport Mechanic (Red Seal 310T)

Canada's supply chain depends entirely on heavy trucks, and those trucks need certified technicians. The transition to diesel emissions aftertreatment (DPF/SCR/DEF) and early fleet electrification adds specialization value for 310T mechanics who stay current on technology.

Welder

Welders are needed in virtually every sector — construction, manufacturing, shipbuilding, oil and gas, and infrastructure. Coded welder certifications (CSA, ASME) command significant wage premiums over standard trade rates. Welder is a single Red Seal trade (456A); red-seal.ca lists no separate welding sub-trades.

$22–$47/hr, median $30 (Job Bank, Canada, 2023–2024, all welders) Cross-sector demand Red Seal designated

Ironworker / Structural

Canada's infrastructure investment — bridges, transit systems, LNG facilities — drives consistent demand for ironworkers. ESDC projects labour demand and supply for ironworkers to be broadly in balance nationally over 2024–2033, and Job Bank's 3-year outlook (updated December 2025) rates prospects as moderate in Ontario, limited in Alberta and very limited in BC.

Automotive Service Technician (Red Seal 310S)

Carpenter

ESDC projects a strong risk of labour shortage for carpenters nationally over 2024–2033, even though BuildForce Canada expects residential construction investment to contract between 2026 and 2028 before growing again. The federal government's housing acceleration targets will sustain this demand well into the 2030s.

Industrial Mechanic (Millwright)

Manufacturing plants, pulp mills, food processing facilities, and power plants all require millwrights to keep industrial machinery running. The Millwright Red Seal (433A) is one of the most versatile trade certifications — nearly every industrial employer across Canada needs one.

$24–$52/hr, median $37 (Job Bank, Canada, 2023–2024) Industrial / manufacturing Red Seal 433A

Why Red Seal Certification Matters More Than Ever

In a tight labour market, Red Seal certification is what separates candidates. Here's why it matters:

  • National mobility: A Red Seal certificate lets you work in any province or territory without additional testing. In a country where job opportunities aren't evenly distributed, this is a career advantage you can't put a dollar value on.
  • Pay: A Statistics Canada study (2012, using 2007 survey data) found that apprentices who completed their program and obtained certification earned about 25% more per hour than those who neither completed nor certified. There is no current official figure for the Red Seal endorsement itself, and Job Bank does not split wages by certification.
  • Employer preference: Large employers — especially resource companies, unionized shops, and government contractors — often require or strongly prefer Red Seal endorsement.
  • Business ownership: In regulated trades, Red Seal certification is often required to operate your own business or qualify for a contractor licence.
  • Career credibility: The Red Seal is Canada's national quality mark for trades. It tells every employer you've met the national standard.

How to Get Started: Apprenticeship Pathway

For the 11 Red Seal trades covered on this site, the path to certification follows the same general structure:

  1. Find an employer sponsor: Apprenticeships start with an employer. Contact companies in your target trade and ask about apprenticeship opportunities.
  2. Register with your provincial authority: Once hired, register with your province's apprenticeship body (AAITA in Alberta, SkilledTradesBC, Skilled Trades Ontario, etc.).
  3. Complete your apprenticeship: 3–5 years of on-the-job training combined with technical school periods.
  4. Pass the Red Seal exam: After achieving journeyperson status or completing your apprenticeship hours, write the Red Seal exam (70% to pass).
  5. Receive your endorsement: Your provincial certificate of qualification receives the Red Seal endorsement — good in all provinces.
