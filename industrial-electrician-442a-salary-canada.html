Red Seal Industrial Electrician (442A) Salary in Canada 2026 — Province by Province

Oil sands, manufacturing, automation, LNG — Job Bank puts the Canada median for industrial electricians at $42.00/hr, above construction electricians ($35.00) and below power system electricians ($46.55) (wages updated November 2025).

Updated September 2026

The 442A Industrial Electrician is one of the better-paid electrical trades in Canada: Job Bank puts its Canada median at $42.00/hr, compared with $35.00/hr for electricians in NOC 72200 (which includes construction electricians) and $46.55/hr for power system electricians (wages updated November 2025). The combination of technical complexity (PLCs, VFDs, instrumentation, high-voltage systems), continuous industrial shift demand, and the physical and lifestyle demands of remote site work creates a market where experienced industrial electricians command among the best trade wages in the country. This guide provides a detailed, province-by-province and industry-by-industry breakdown of what 442A Industrial Electricians actually earn in 2026.

Most pay figures on this page are Government of Canada Job Bank data for everyone working in the occupation, not only certified tradespeople. The 442A exam is 100 questions in 4 hours and the pass mark is 70%, which is 70 correct.

Province-by-Province Salary Breakdown

Alberta — $25.00–$68.00/hr, median $44.00 (Job Bank, wages updated November 2025)

Alberta has the highest provincial upper pay figure for industrial electricians in Canada (Job Bank high $68.00/hr, wages updated November 2025), although Saskatchewan ($49.00/hr) and British Columbia ($45.00/hr) have higher medians than Alberta ($44.00/hr). The oil sands operations north of Fort McMurray — Syncrude Mildred Lake, Suncor Millennium, CNRL Horizon, Cenovus Christina Lake, MEG Energy — all maintain large industrial electrician workforces for maintenance and capital projects. Direct-hire maintenance industrial electricians at major oil sands operators are paid on each operator's own wage scale or collective agreement; benefits, pension and RRSP matching vary by employer. Turnaround (shutdown maintenance) work through maintenance contractors often adds camp accommodation, meals and overtime; Job Bank's Wood Buffalo–Cold Lake region (Fort McMurray area) range for industrial electricians is $26.95–$63.09/hr, median $48.00 (wages updated November 2025). The Alberta Industrial Heartland (Strathcona County, Fort Saskatchewan) chemical and refining complex, in the Edmonton area, also employs large numbers of industrial electricians; Job Bank's Edmonton Region range for industrial electricians is $25.00–$68.00/hr, median $44.00 (wages updated November 2025). In Edmonton and Calgary, where general manufacturing also employs industrial electricians, Job Bank's regional median is $44.00/hr in both (Calgary Region range $34.00–$62.00/hr, wages updated November 2025) — slightly above the $42.00/hr Canada median.

British Columbia — $35.90–$54.95/hr, median $45.00 (Job Bank, wages updated November 2025)

BC industrial electrician wages are driven primarily by the northern resource sector and LNG. Kitimat-area LNG Canada facility maintenance employs industrial electricians, with camp and travel allowances on remote rotations (Job Bank's British Columbia high for industrial electricians is $54.95/hr, wages updated November 2025). Mining operations in the BC Interior (copper, gold, silver) employ site maintenance industrial electricians, and remote mine sites may provide accommodation; Job Bank's industrial electrician medians in Interior regions include $47.92/hr in the Cariboo Region, $46.00/hr in the Thompson–Okanagan Region and $45.81/hr in the Kootenay Region (wages updated November 2025). Metro Vancouver manufacturing also employs industrial electricians; Job Bank's Lower Mainland–Southwest range for industrial electricians is $28.00–$53.00/hr, median $46.50 (wages updated November 2025). Pulp and paper mills (Canfor, West Fraser, Paper Excellence) employ mill maintenance industrial electricians. BC Hydro and FortisBC facilities also employ electricians; for comparison, Job Bank's British Columbia median for power system electricians (NOC 72202) is $50.99/hr (wages updated November 2025).

Ontario — $29.00–$48.00/hr, median $40.50 (Job Bank, wages updated November 2025)

Ontario's industrial electrician wages are driven by advanced manufacturing rather than resource extraction. Automotive manufacturing (GM Oshawa, Ford Oakville, Stellantis Windsor, Honda Alliston) and their Tier 1 suppliers employ large numbers of industrial electricians for plant maintenance and automation. Chemical and pharmaceutical manufacturing in Sarnia, the GTA, and southwestern Ontario also employs process-industrial electricians. Job Bank's Ontario range for industrial electricians is $29.00–$48.00/hr, median $40.50 (wages updated November 2025). IBEW locals in Ontario represent industrial electricians in many plant settings. Steel mills such as ArcelorMittal Dofasco in Hamilton and Algoma Steel in Sault Ste. Marie also employ journeyperson industrial electricians, and automation integration roles in Ontario's manufacturing corridor value strong PLC skills.

Saskatchewan — $38.00–$57.78/hr, median $49.00 (Job Bank, wages updated November 2025)

Saskatchewan industrial electricians benefit from the potash mining sector (Nutrien, Mosaic), uranium mining (Cameco, Orano), and oil production (Crescent Point, Whitecap). Potash mine industrial electricians are paid on each operator's wage scale or collective agreement; benefits and pension vary by employer. Uranium mine positions in northern Saskatchewan (McClean Lake, McArthur River area) involve fly-in/fly-out rotations; Job Bank's Saskatchewan range for industrial electricians is $38.00–$57.78/hr, median $49.00 (wages updated November 2025). The Lloydminster area heavy oil sector pays near-Alberta rates. General manufacturing also employs industrial electricians in Regina and Saskatoon; Job Bank's Regina–Moose Mountain range is $40.87–$60.00/hr, median $44.00, and its Saskatoon–Biggar range is $38.00–$57.78/hr, median $49.00 (wages updated November 2025).

Industry Sector Wage Analysis

Oil and Gas — The Premium Sector

Oil and gas — both upstream (production) and downstream (refining, petrochemicals) — is the highest-paying sector for industrial electricians in Canada. The reasons are straightforward: remote locations (Fort McMurray, Peace River country, Kitimat), continuous 24/7 operations that require shift coverage, hazardous area electrical requirements (Class I, Zone 0/1/2 classified area work under CEC Section 18 and IEC 60079 series), high-consequence equipment (motors driving pumps, compressors, and fans that are costly to repair or replace), and zero-downtime expectations where a production stoppage can be very costly.

Oil sands industrial electricians can add camp accommodation, meals, travel allowances, overtime and rotation premiums to their hourly rate; the total depends on the employer, rotation and agreement. For reference, Job Bank's Wood Buffalo–Cold Lake region (Fort McMurray area) range for industrial electricians is $26.95–$63.09/hr, median $48.00 (wages updated November 2025).

Manufacturing — Stable with Automation Premium

Manufacturing industrial electricians' pay depends on location and employer; Job Bank's Canada range for industrial electricians is $28.00–$54.00/hr, median $42.00 (wages updated November 2025). The ongoing trend toward industrial automation (robot integration, smart manufacturing, Industry 4.0 systems) is creating a significant wage premium for industrial electricians who develop strong PLC, robotics, and automation skills. Automotive plants that have converted to robotic assembly lines need industrial electricians who can diagnose and maintain not just motors and drives but also robotic arms (Fanuc, KUKA, ABB), vision systems, and complex PLC/SCADA networks. These automation-focused positions are in demand; for reference, Job Bank's Ontario range for industrial electricians is $29.00–$48.00/hr, median $40.50 (wages updated November 2025).

Utilities and Power Generation

Electrical utility industrial electricians — working on power generation equipment, substations, and distribution infrastructure — typically have excellent benefit packages; for comparison, Job Bank's Canada median for power system electricians (NOC 72202) is $46.55/hr (range $36.26–$60.00, wages updated November 2025). BC Hydro, Ontario Power Generation, Hydro-Québec (indirectly via contractors), and TransAlta employ industrial electricians for maintenance, testing, and commissioning of power generation and transmission equipment. Work includes high-voltage switching, transformer maintenance, generator maintenance, protective relay testing, and switchgear inspection. These positions are typically unionized (IBEW) and offer exceptional job security, defined-benefit pensions, and regular hours — a strong lifestyle alternative to oil sands rotations for comparable wages.

Pulp, Paper, and Forestry

BC's and Ontario's pulp and paper mills are major industrial electrician employers; Job Bank's industrial electrician medians are $45.00/hr in British Columbia and $40.50/hr in Ontario (wages updated November 2025). Mill work involves large AC and DC drives, high-voltage motors on chip refiners and paper machines, complex DCS (Distributed Control System) networks, and extensive process instrumentation. Mill shutdowns (annual maintenance periods of 1–3 weeks) generate overtime and turnaround bonuses. Forestry sector mills in BC's Interior and northern Ontario provide fly-in or drive-in positions with accommodation — effective wages including accommodation are competitive with major city industrial rates.

Career Earnings Progression

A strategic career path for a 442A Industrial Electrician in Canada:

  1. Apprenticeship (4 periods in Alberta): pay rises each period as a set percentage of the journeyperson rate; Alberta's Electrician program, which covers industrial electrician training, sets minimums of 50%, 60%, 70% and 80% (Alberta Tradesecrets, checked September 2026)
  2. Early Journeyperson (Years 4–7): manufacturing or plant maintenance (Job Bank Canada median for industrial electricians: $42.00/hr, wages updated November 2025) — focus on building PLC, VFD, and instrumentation skills; potentially pursue high-voltage qualification
  3. Experienced JM with Specialty Skills (Years 7–12): oil and gas or automation roles (Job Bank's Alberta high for industrial electricians is $68.00/hr, wages updated November 2025) — earning potential peaks with specialty qualifications and experience
  4. Senior/Specialist (Years 12+): roles as automation specialist, reliability technician, or independent contractor; or transition to automation engineer, maintenance manager, or IBEW representative roles
Electrification trend: The industrial electrification trend — replacing diesel-powered equipment with electric motors and drives, adding EV charging infrastructure to industrial facilities, and integrating renewable generation with industrial power systems — is creating new demand for industrial electricians with power electronics and energy management skills. This emerging specialization will likely become a significant premium-earning area for industrial electricians over the next decade.

LNG and Hydrogen — Emerging High-Pay Sectors

Two emerging sectors are creating new high-wage opportunities for 442A Industrial Electricians in Canada:

LNG Facilities

LNG Canada's Kitimat facility and proposed additional LNG terminals on BC's coast require large industrial electrician workforces for both construction commissioning and ongoing operations maintenance. LNG facility electrical work involves cryogenic-rated equipment, classified area wiring in hydrogen-rich and flammable gas environments, high-voltage switchgear for compressor drives, and extensive instrumentation and safety system integration. LNG operations roles often include camp accommodation; Job Bank's British Columbia range for industrial electricians is $35.90–$54.95/hr, median $45.00 (wages updated November 2025).

Hydrogen and Clean Energy

Canada's emerging green hydrogen and clean ammonia sector — driven by federal clean energy investment tax credits — will require industrial electricians for electrolysis plant construction and operation. Alberta's hydrogen hub ambitions and BC's clean energy projects will create demand for industrial electricians with power electronics and large-scale electrolyzer experience. This sector is in early stages but will represent a growing employment category through 2030 and beyond.
