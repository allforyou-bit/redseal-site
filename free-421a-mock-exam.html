The 367-question bank, area by area

At 367 questions this is the largest bank on the site, roughly triple most trades here, because the trade's scope runs from a diesel injector to a high-voltage battery pack. Here is how those 367 divide, and where marks go missing.

  • Diesel Engine — 67 questions Four-stroke theory, air intake and charge air, common rail and HEUI fuel systems, injector operation and cylinder cut-out testing, valve lash, cooling and lubrication, and aftertreatment — EGR, DPF regeneration, DEF and SCR. Aftertreatment is where marks disappear: a derate gets answered as a mechanical failure when the fault is a sensor or DEF quality, and low-power causes get guessed rather than split into air, fuel and back pressure.
  • Hydraulic Systems — 64 questions Gear, vane and piston pumps, load-sensing and pressure-compensated circuits, directional and relief valves, counterbalance valves and pilot-operated checks, cylinders, motors, accumulators and filtration. Two things recur: counterbalance function on a boom that drifts down, and schematics — reading a dashed pilot line as a working line changes the answer.
  • Electrical Systems — 62 questions Series and parallel circuits, battery state of charge and load testing, charging and starting systems, relays, sensors, wiring diagrams and J1939 multiplexed networks. The habit that costs marks is a resistance check where a live test belongs — voltage drop under load finds the corroded connection an ohmmeter calls good.
  • Powertrain — 49 questions Clutches, torque converters, powershift and hydrostatic transmissions, differentials and final drives, driveshafts and planetary gear sets. Planetary questions are the ones people re-read three times: which member is held, which drives, which outputs, which way it turns. Stall-test interpretation runs second.
  • Brakes & Steering — 46 questions Air brake systems, wet-disc and oil-cooled service brakes, spring-applied parking brakes, accumulators in brake circuits, articulated steering, orbital steering units and cylinders. Technicians who learned brakes on a highway truck carry over assumptions that do not hold — an oil-cooled brake pack is nothing like an S-cam foundation brake.
  • Safety & Tools — 25 questions Lockout, blocking and cribbing a machine, stored energy in accumulators and suspensions, articulation-joint locks, hoisting components, WHMIS, torque procedure and precision measuring tools. The one that catches people is discharging a hydraulic accumulator before opening the circuit.
  • HVAC & Cab Comfort — 20 questions The refrigeration cycle, refrigerant handling and recovery, compressors and clutches, thermal expansion valve versus orifice tube systems, cab heaters and cab filtration. Gauge diagnosis is the sticking point: the pattern for a restriction gets answered as an overcharge.
  • Structures & Attachments — 20 questions Frames and undercarriage, booms, sticks and buckets, pins and bushings, wear parts and ground engaging tools, couplers, crack inspection. Marks go on wear measurement limits and on repair sequence — preheat and welding procedure on high-strength steel are not eyeball work.
  • Hybrid & Electric Equipment — 9 questions High-voltage safety and PPE, isolation and de-energizing procedure, orange cable identification, battery packs and thermal management, motor-generators and regenerative systems. Nine questions most people skip — procedure recall, the cheapest marks here.
  • Preventive Maintenance — 5 questions Service intervals, fluid and filter specification, greasing and scheduled oil sampling. The one real skill is reading an oil analysis report — knowing which wear metal points at which component.

Getting a real reading out of 50 questions

The Red Seal exam for this trade runs 135 questions in a four-hour writing time — under two minutes each. Sit the 50 in 90 minutes, closed book, and read the result as a diagnosis.

  • Answer the question, do not fix the machine The occupational habit here is to keep diagnosing. Exam questions ask one thing — the next step, the likely cause, the correct spec — and technicians talk themselves out of the right answer by adding conditions the question never gave.
  • Dot every answer you guessed, as you go On a bank this wide you will guess in the systems you rarely touch, and a lucky guess hides that gap.
  • Score by area, not by total Forty out of fifty means nothing on its own. Ten misses all in hydraulics is a week of schematics; ten spread evenly across ten areas is a pace problem, and the fix is timed practice, not theory.
  • Then study only the misses and the dots Read the explanation in the back, then redraw the circuit or the power flow by hand. Schematic and diagnostic-sequence questions repay drawing; recall items do not.

What the Red Seal exam for Heavy Duty Equipment Technician covers

The Red Seal Program publishes the breakdown for this trade: 135 questions across nine blocks — Common occupational skills (10), Engines and supporting systems (20), Steering, suspension, brake, undercarriage and wheel systems (16), Electrical and electronic systems (25), Drivetrain systems (17), Environmental control systems (9), Hydraulic, hydrostatic and pneumatic systems (25), Structural components, operator stations and attachments (9), and Hybrid and all-electric equipment (4) (red-seal.ca exam information). Its Exam Preparation Guide states four choices per question with one correct answer, a 70% pass mark and a four-hour writing time.

Our areas are not the official block names, but the weighting lands close: electrical and hydraulics are the two largest blocks on that list and the second and third largest in our bank. Our Brakes & Steering covers what the official list bundles as steering, suspension, brakes, undercarriage and wheels; our Safety & Tools and Preventive Maintenance sit inside common occupational skills. Your provincial or territorial apprenticeship authority administers the exam and rules on eligibility and scheduling.

What 421A apprentices ask

  • Why is the 421A bank so much bigger than the other trades here? Because the scope is. One technician works across diesel engines, hydraulics, electronics, drivetrain, brakes, HVAC, structures and now high-voltage systems. At 120 questions whole systems would go untested.
  • Does it cover on-highway trucks? No. The questions stay on off-road equipment — excavators, dozers, loaders, graders and the engines and drivetrains in them. Highway truck material belongs to the truck and transport trade; if that is your work, the 310T quiz is the right one.
  • Do I need air brake knowledge for this? Air brakes are in the bank because the trade covers them, alongside oil-cooled brakes highway work never touches. Whether your province also requires a separate air brake certificate is a question for your apprenticeship authority.
  • Only nine hybrid and electric questions — worth studying? Yes, and they are the easiest nine here. Isolation, PPE and cable identification are procedure recall rather than diagnosis, and the block appears on the official breakdown too.
