Where the 139 questions sit — and what trips apprentices up

The free PDF pulls 50 questions from the same 139-question 310S bank, so the balance below is roughly what you will see. Each area lists what it covers and where marks get lost.

  • Brakes — 28 questions Hydraulic fundamentals and pedal diagnosis, master cylinders and proportioning, disc and drum service, brake fluid specification, and the wheel-speed inputs an anti-lock system depends on. Two errors repeat: quoting the minimum thickness stamped on a rotor as the machine-to figure, when it is the discard dimension; and calling every ABS lamp a failed module, when a sensor circuit, a damaged tone ring or a chafed harness is far more likely. Fluid questions expect you to know why DOT 5 silicone does not belong in a DOT 3 or DOT 4 system.
  • Engine — 27 questions Four-stroke operation, valve train and timing, cooling and lubrication, fuel delivery, forced induction, emission control, and the reasoning behind common trouble codes. The classic miss is reading a lean code as "replace the oxygen sensor" — the sensor is reporting honestly, and the real fault is unmetered air or not enough fuel volume. The second is not being able to say what a compression test tells you that a leak-down test does not: one gives a number, the other tells you where the air is going.
  • Electrical — 27 questions Ohm's law applied to real circuits, series and parallel behaviour, relay and switch control logic, starting and charging tests, parasitic draw, meter and scope technique, and module communication over the data bus. The most common error is taking a resistance reading where the fault calls for a voltage-drop test under load — a corroded terminal reads a fraction of an ohm cold and still drops two volts carrying current.
  • Transmission and driveline — 25 questions Automatic transmission hydraulics, planetary gear sets and ratio reasoning, torque converter lock-up, clutch release, transfer cases, differentials, universal and CV joints, and driveline angle. Apprentices blame every shudder on converter lock-up when driveline angle, a tired U-joint or a misfire under load gives the same complaint. Fluid questions are marks people give away: transmission fluids are not generic, and the wrong one changes friction characteristics and shift quality.
  • Suspension and steering — 22 questions Alignment angles, springs, struts and dampers, bushings and control arms, wheel bearings, rack-and-pinion and recirculating-ball steering, power assist, tire wear patterns and pressure monitoring. The recurring confusion is which angles wear tires: toe and camber do, caster does not on its own — it is a stability and steering-return angle. A pull is more often unequal camber than caster, and a feathered edge points at toe.
  • Heating and air conditioning — 10 questions The smallest block, and the cheapest marks to collect: component function, pressure-temperature relationships, recovery and charging practice, blend-door and airflow control, and the electrical side of climate control. Where people slip is assuming every weak-cooling complaint is a low charge, when a stuck blend door, a plugged cabin filter or a dead condenser fan gives the customer the same symptom.

How to actually use 50 questions

Red Seal allows four hours for the 125-question Automotive Service Technician examination — a shade under two minutes a question. Set a 95-minute timer, sit all 50 in one go, and keep the manuals shut. Put a small G beside every answer you were not sure of, including the ones you narrowed to two and picked.

When you mark it, count a guessed-right answer as a miss. It is not knowledge yet. Then tally the misses by topic, not by question number: one wrong answer in HVAC means little, but four wrong in the brake questions is a pattern worth a weekend. Brakes, engine and electrical are 82 of the 139 questions in the full bank, so misses clustered there are where the effort pays.

Read only the explanations for the ones you got wrong, then re-sit the same 50 cold a week later. The second score is the one that means something.

What the 310S exam is built from

The exam is built from the Red Seal Occupational Standard for Automotive Service Technician published at red-seal.ca, not from any textbook. Red Seal states the interprovincial examination for this trade has 125 questions, the writing time is four hours, the pass mark is 70%, and every question is multiple choice with four choices and one correct answer.

The standard divides the trade into major work activities with a published weighting for each. The heaviest is steering, suspension, braking and control systems, tires, hubs and wheel bearings at 19%, then electrical and comfort control systems at 18% and engine and engine support systems at 17%. That is why this bank leans on brakes, electrical and engine.

Our six topic areas are study groupings, not the official major work activities — module communication, restraint and body systems, and hybrid and electric vehicle systems carry their own weightings, so read the published standard alongside any practice set. Eligibility and booking are set by your provincial or territorial apprenticeship authority.

310S questions we get asked

  • Are these the real 310S exam questions? No. Every question here is original, written against the topic areas of the occupational standard. Live exam questions are secure and are written and reviewed by tradespeople through the Red Seal Program.
  • Why are there nearly three times as many brake questions as HVAC questions? Because the trade standard weights them that way. Braking, steering and suspension carry the largest single share of the exam, while comfort control is one part of a broader electrical activity.
  • I work at a dealership on one make. Is that enough? The exam covers the trade as practised across Canada, not the vehicles in your bay. If your shop never sees a manual transmission, a transfer case or a drum brake, those are exactly the questions to test yourself on.
  • Can I use a calculator? The Red Seal exam preparation guide lists a calculator among the supplies provided if one is needed, along with the exam booklet, reference material where required, and a bubble sheet. Practise gear-ratio arithmetic without one anyway.
