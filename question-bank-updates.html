Question Bank Updates

A dated record of what was corrected in the practice question banks, in the PDFs made from them, and in the exam facts stated on this site.

Updated October 2026

How corrections are made

In September 2026 all eleven question banks were audited. Each finding was put to a reviewer whose job was to defend the question, and most of the findings that survived were checked again by a second, independent reviewer.

  • Wrong or disputable answer: the question is removed. It is not edited until it passes. Removed questions leave the free quiz, the free pages and the PDFs.
  • Wrong explanation: the explanation is corrected. The corrected line is written from a source the corrector opened and is checked by an independent verifier before it goes live. The question, its options and its answer stay the same.
  • New questions go live only after a break attempt and a source check. They replace a question that tested the same fact as another, or bring a bank back to its official exam length. A reviewer tries to break each one and its source is checked independently; candidates that fail are dropped, not revised.
  • Findings that are not defects are dismissed. Those questions are left as they were.

Later corrections are added to this page with their date. To report an error, use the Contact page.

421A Heavy Duty Equipment Technician

Bank size: 421 before the audit, 406 after.

  • 2026-10-16 1 question replaced and 6 explanations or key concepts corrected. Torque converter: the old question had coupling begin at about 90% of impeller speed; the new question in its place asks what happens as turbine speed comes close to pump speed (the stator freewheels and the converter works as a fluid coupling), as Allison’s operator manuals describe it, with no fixed percentage. Spring brakes: the explanation called 90 psi a minimum and said the brakes release at 60 psi and fully release at 90 psi or more, with no source; it now gives Ontario’s air brake handbook (about 60 psi releases them) and SGI’s air brake manual (70 psi or more holds them released, they may drag below about 70 psi and they fully apply between 20 and 45 psi), so the 90 psi in the answer is enough to release them. Supplemental coolant additive: a single range of 1.5–3.0 units per gallon, tested at every service, was removed because the range depends on the additive and the coolant; the explanation now gives Cummins’ statements that under-concentration leads to liner pitting and over-concentration to additive drop-out, plugged radiator tubes and overheating. Starting circuit voltage drop: the limits are now the makers’ (Hino: under 0.2 V on the positive circuit while cranking; Delco Remy: more than 0.5 V for the positive and negative sides together at a 500 A carbon-pile load is too high). Charging circuit voltage drop: the test is now described as Delco Remy prints it, with a carbon pile loading the circuit to the alternator’s rated output and both sides added together, in place of an engine-speed test with limits that had no source. Wording that a topic appears regularly on safety exams and that a step is often skipped was removed.
  • 2026-09-14 7 explanations corrected in the hybrid and electric equipment questions: one from the audit list and six found when the rest of that block was re-checked. No question, option or answer changed.

306A Plumber

Bank size: 135 before the audit, 130 after.

  • 2026-10-16 3 explanations corrected; one of the three questions was also reworded. Options and answers are unchanged. Bathroom exhaust fan: the explanation said a fan rated exactly 50 CFM sits right on the 25 L/s minimum of National Building Code Article 9.32.3.7; 50 CFM is about 23.6 L/s and falls short (about 53 CFM is needed). It also said British Columbia dropped its 23 L/s intermittent and 9 L/s continuous figures, but BC’s 2024 Building Code keeps them, and a US ASHRAE figure was removed. The question now names the 2020 and 2025 editions of the National Building Code, and the check on delivered airflow is tied to the fan’s rating at a set external static pressure (25 Pa for this fan, Article 9.32.3.10). Water heater supply: the explanation said the plumbing code sets no minimum size; National Plumbing Code Sentence 2.6.3.4.(4) sets NPS 3/4 for the supply to a water heater that serves more than one fixture, and the tank connection size is now quoted from water heater makers’ manuals. Fill valve: a fill valve that will not shut off was described as phantom flushing; it gives a running toilet, and a toilet that refills on its own is losing water, past the flapper or flush valve or through a refill tube pushed too far into the overflow pipe (Fluidmaster). Wording about what the exam often asks and which errors are common was removed from all three.
  • 2026-09-13 5 questions removed: one asked about a wall-hung water closet and answered with the floor-mount procedure; four more were removed after two independent reviews.

442A Industrial Electrician

Bank size: 135 before the audit, 130 after.

  • 2026-10-16 1 question replaced, 3 questions reworded with their explanations corrected, and 7 other explanations or key concepts corrected. Service factor: the old question keyed “no action needed” for a motor drawing 46 A against a 42 A nameplate with a service factor of 1.15, by multiplying the full-load current by the service factor; service factor is a multiplier on rated power (NEMA MG1, as EASA quotes it), and the new question in its place asks which statement about the motor is correct: its winding runs hotter and its insulation life is shortened. Motor protection: overload settings of 125% and 115% of full-load current, which were confirmed only for the 2018 edition of the Canadian Electrical Code, were removed from four questions; the 125% minimum for the branch-circuit conductors of a continuous-duty motor is now quoted from Rule 28-106 1) of the 2024 edition and stated the same way in the three questions that use it; and branch-circuit overcurrent protection is described from Rule 28-200 and Table 29. Motor nameplate: the options were rewritten so that the answer is no longer much shorter than the others. Polarization index: the question now states Class F insulation, and the minimums are given by insulation class (1.5 for Class A, 2.0 for Class B and higher; IEEE 43 as quoted by test-instrument makers and ANSI/EASA AR100). PLC scan time: the question asked about program scan time, which is one part of the scan, and a typical range of 1–100 ms with no source was removed. Approved equipment (Section 2): a list of certification marks, a statement about UL marks without a small “c” and an Ontario regulation number had no source; the explanation now uses the Electrical Safety Authority’s bulletin wording. Harmonic filters: the explanation that listed drives among the loads that overheat neutral conductors (noted as under review on 2026-10-15) now says that balanced three-phase drives produce no triplen harmonics (Rockwell Automation). Direct-on-line starting current is now ABB’s figure, usually 6 to 8 times rated current, not 5–8 times. Wording that a topic is often tested on the exam or that an error is the usual or most frequent one was removed from three explanations.
  • 2026-09-13 5 questions removed: one had two defensible answers, and four more were removed after two independent reviews.

313A Refrigeration & Air Conditioning Mechanic

Bank size: 116 before the audit, 115 after.

  • 2026-10-16 1 question replaced and 7 explanations or key concepts corrected. R-22 to R-407C retrofit: the old question put low suction pressure after the conversion down to a TXV that needs recalibration or replacement; Arkema’s published retrofit data lists no TXV change-out for R-407C, and the new question in its place asks which statement agrees with that data; the answer is that the TXV stays, and the explanation adds that POE oil is required and that the charge is about 90–95% of the R-22 charge. Five explanations or key concepts gave superheat of 8–12°F, subcooling of 10–15°F or a condensing temperature 20–30°F above ambient as typical, with no source; they now point to the figure the equipment manufacturer specifies, two of them with Danfoss and Trane figures as examples, and the key concept on TXV hunting now adds that a bulb that has lost its charge does not hunt: the valve closes and the coil starves. An unsourced sentence about R-466A was removed, and the refrigerant handling card is now described as Ontario’s (an approved one-day course offered through HRAI and its delivery partners, renewed through HRAI) rather than as a card used across most of the country.

456A Welder

Bank size: 120 before the audit, 125 after.

  • 2026-10-16 1 question reworded and 5 explanations corrected. Low-hydrogen electrode storage: the answer gave one oven range, 120–150°C (250–300°F), but holding temperatures differ by product (Hobart gives 250–300°F for 7018 XLM and 220–350°F for 418); the question now says the package has been opened, and the answer is an oven held at the electrode maker’s temperature. E7018: a fixed 120–150°C storage temperature and a 4-hour limit out of the oven were replaced with the maker’s figures, a claim that E7018 is the go-to electrode for structural steel was removed, and the box marking is now quoted from a Hobart data sheet (AWS A5.1 E7018 H4R, AWS A5.1M E4918, CWB E4918-1-H4). Carbon equivalent: the key concept said preheat is required above a CE of 0.45; CE is one input, with thickness, hydrogen level, restraint and process, to the preheat the WPS or WPDS specifies. Welder position tests: the AWS D1.1 ranges are now taken from the CWB’s comparison of CSA W47.1 with D1.1 (2015 edition). Tungsten colours: the statement that the 2009 edition of AWS A5.12 moved ceriated tungsten from orange to grey, and the current types given for the thoriated and lanthanated electrodes, had no source; the colours now follow tungsten makers’ charts (grey for 2% ceriated, formerly orange). Intermittent welds: pitch is measured centre to centre, now cited to Miller’s guide to weld symbols. Wording that called an error common or classic, or said a change catches people out on the exam, was removed from three explanations.
  • 2026-09-13 1 question removed: the two independent reviewers disagreed, and a third review found a second option defensible (a neutral flame for aluminum).

Exam facts and site pages

  • 2026-10-16 The Gasfitter common-mistakes guide taught the four vent categories by vent pressure and by flue gas temperature above or below the dew point (the 2026-10-06 entry below describes Category II the same way). It now uses the basis CSA B149.1 defines them by, as Alberta’s STANDATA interpretation quotes it, and the basis the Gasfitter question bank has used for its vent-category question since the same day: a non-positive or positive vent static pressure, and a flue loss of 17% or more or less than 17%. A statement with no source that 90%+ efficiency furnaces are almost always Category IV was removed, and so was a rule of thumb for vent materials (PVC for Category IV, double-wall metal for Category I, stainless for Category III), which Saskatchewan’s B149.1:25 interpretations contradict for condensing boilers; the guide now says to take the vent material from the category on the appliance’s certification label and the maker’s installation instructions.
  • 2026-10-16 Corrections after an independent re-check of the general-guide changes listed below. The apprenticeship-length guide now gives the minimum wages Manitoba’s regulations set for each of its five trades (for example 135% of the provincial minimum wage in the first level, rising to 195%, for automotive service technicians and truck and transport mechanics, and 40% to 80% of a construction reference wage for construction electricians), not only the general regulation’s 110% to 150%, which is the floor; its BC badges say 4 years rather than “over 4 years”; and it names Alberta’s Apprenticeship and Industry Training, whose website is Tradesecrets, rather than calling Tradesecrets an apprenticeship authority. The trade-comparison guide no longer says 442A has the highest wage ceiling of its eight trades: Alberta’s $68.00 an hour is the highest provincial high wage among them, but the Canada-wide high for industrial electricians ($54.00) is below those for refrigeration and air conditioning mechanics ($56.00) and heavy-duty equipment mechanics ($55.00). The Red Seal vs provincial certification guide no longer says that a certificate without a Red Seal is valid only in the province that issued it or is not portable (under the Canadian Free Trade Agreement, skilled workers in a regulated trade can work in another province with or without the endorsement, and Alberta recognizes certificates from other provinces and territories either way), that the Red Seal is recognized in every province and territory (that depends on where the trade is designated Red Seal: seven provinces for Ironworker (Generalist)), or that you pass a provincial exam and then write the Red Seal exam (most provinces and territories use the Red Seal exam as the final certification exam in Red Seal trades); it no longer gives gas fitters as an example of a compulsory trade or treats designated and compulsory trades as the same thing, it gives the program’s formal name, the Interprovincial Standards Red Seal Program, and its visible FAQ now matches its search markup. Demand claims with no source next to Job Bank figures were replaced with Job Bank’s outlooks or removed: on the plumber career and salary guides, including the career guide’s page summaries (a moderate risk of labour shortage nationally over 2024–2033, and 2025–2027 outlooks for the provinces from Good in New Brunswick, Nova Scotia and Prince Edward Island to Very limited in Quebec), on the trade-comparison guide’s 310T, 309A, 306A and 442A cards, on the trades guide’s 313A card (a strong risk of shortage nationally), and in the trades-in-demand guide’s tags for ironworkers and carpenters, which had said “Infrastructure-driven” and “Housing boom driven” (ESDC projects labour demand and supply broadly in line for ironworkers, and BuildForce Canada expects residential construction investment to contract from 2026 to 2028); for the same reason, the plumber career guide no longer says the construction boom will sustain plumbing demand through the late 2020s. The trade-comparison guide also no longer describes the truck mechanic’s work by truck classes 6 to 8 and no longer promises top-tier wages for welding and industrial pipefitting specialties or a strong welding specialty premium, none of which had a source, and the plumber career guide now says that Alberta’s Plumber program includes Gasfitter – Class B certification, where it had said Alberta plumbers may also pursue the separate Gas Fitter trades.
