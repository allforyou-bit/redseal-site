About the 442A Red Seal Exam — Industrial Electrician

The 442A Industrial Electrician Red Seal exam is Canada's national certification for electricians who install, maintain, and troubleshoot electrical systems in industrial plants, manufacturing facilities, oil and gas operations, and processing plants. The 442A is distinct from the 309A (Construction Electrician) — it focuses on high-voltage equipment, motor drives, PLCs, process instrumentation, and industrial automation rather than building construction wiring.

The exam consists of 100 multiple-choice questions based on the 2023 Red Seal Occupational Standard (RSOS), aligned with the Canadian Electrical Code Part I (CSA C22.1), with a passing score of 70%. Exam time is up to 4 hours.

Official 442A Exam Breakdown (red-seal.ca)

Red Seal publishes how the 100 questions on the real 442A exam are distributed across its Major Work Activities:

  • A — Performs common occupational skills — 9 questions (9%)
  • B — Installs and maintains generating, distribution and service systems — 23 questions (23%)
  • C — Installs and maintains wiring systems — 20 questions (20%)
  • D — Installs and maintains rotating and non-rotating equipment and control systems — 21 questions (21%)
  • E — Installs and maintains signalling and communication systems — 10 questions (10%)
  • F — Installs and maintains process control systems — 17 questions (17%)

Every question is four-option multiple choice with one correct answer, the writing time is four hours and the pass mark is 70%. Source: 442A exam information, red-seal.ca. Exams are periodically rewritten to updated occupational standards, so check that page before you book.

What this question bank covers

These are the topic areas in this practice bank and what each one covers. The real 442A exam has 100 questions and a four-hour writing time. How those questions are distributed across the official blocks is published by Red Seal — see the 442A exam information page.

  • Motors & Motor Controls — The largest and most heavily tested block. Three-phase induction motor theory, starting methods (DOL, star-delta, autotransformer, soft starter), motor protection (overloads, thermistors), reversing starters, and reduced-voltage starting calculations. Expect questions on motor nameplate data interpretation.
  • Variable Frequency Drives (VFDs) & Power Electronics — VFD parameter programming (V/Hz, sensorless vector, closed-loop), harmonic distortion and line reactors, DC bus voltage, braking resistors, encoder feedback, and common VFD fault codes.
  • Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) — Ladder logic (contacts, coils, timers, counters), I/O module wiring, analog vs. discrete signals (4–20 mA, 0–10V), PLC diagnostics, and safety relay/SIL applications. Understanding ladder logic is essential — questions include diagrams.
  • Process Instrumentation & Control — Sensors and transmitters (thermocouples, RTDs, pressure transmitters, flow meters), 4–20 mA loop power, HART protocol, loop calibration, and PID controller tuning (proportional, integral, derivative).
  • Electrical Theory & Power Systems — Three-phase power calculations, power factor correction (capacitor banks), transformer connections (delta-wye, wye-delta), harmonics, and power quality. Medium-voltage distribution basics (4160V, 13.8 kV switchgear).
  • CEC Part I — Industrial Applications & Safety — Hazardous location classification (Division 1/2, Zone 0/1/2), explosion-proof enclosure requirements, arc flash protection (CSA Z462), lockout/tagout procedures, and industrial grounding requirements.

Exam Strategy for 442A

Ladder logic is non-negotiable. PLC ladder logic diagrams appear in multiple 442A questions — you need to trace circuit logic, predict output states, and identify faults from a diagram. Practice reading rungs with seal-in circuits, timers, and counters until it's intuitive.

Motors + VFDs = 43% of the exam. Together, the motor controls and VFD blocks make up nearly half the exam. Focus here first. Understand the relationship between motor speed, frequency, and torque in VFD operation.

Hazardous locations come up reliably. Know Division vs. Zone classification, T-code temperature ratings, and which enclosure types (EX, XP, PX) are permitted in each area classification.
