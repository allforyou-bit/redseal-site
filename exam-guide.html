Red Seal Exam Registration Guide

How to register for your Red Seal exam in every province — fees, steps, and contacts.

Updated July 2026 All 13 provinces & territories Fees verified against official sources

How Red Seal exam registration works

There is no national registration desk: the Red Seal exam is always booked through your provincial or territorial apprenticeship authority. The process — and the fee — differs by jurisdiction, but the shape is the same everywhere. Two pathways lead to the same interprovincial exam: completing an apprenticeship, or proving enough verified work experience as a trade qualifier / challenger (most jurisdictions require 1.0–1.5× the apprenticeship's hours).

1. Confirm your eligibility. Apprentices: finish all levels and required hours. Challengers: gather proof of hours (pay stubs, employer declarations, journeyperson verification) matching your province's rule — see your province's card below.
2. Apply to your authority. Submit the application (online portals in most provinces now) with documentation and the fee. Processing can take 2–8 weeks — apply well before you want to write.
3. Book your sitting. Once approved you'll get an authorization to schedule at an approved exam centre. Rural candidates: check travel dates early; some centres run monthly.
4. Prepare with the real blueprint. Every trade's question count and topic weighting is published — our study guide and free question banks are built around them.
5. Exam day. Bring government photo ID and your confirmation. Phones, smartwatches and notes stay outside. Pass mark is 70% everywhere; results typically arrive in 2–6 weeks.

Good news first: where exams are cheap or free right now

🎉 Newfoundland & Labrador: $0. All apprentice and trade-qualifier exam fees were eliminated on April 1, 2025 — writing and rewriting are free.
🎉 Ontario: first attempt free for apprentices completing their Ontario program on or after Jan 1, 2025 (written certifying exam).
🎉 Nova Scotia: trade-qualifier fees waived for construction trades until October 2026 — worth roughly $740 per candidate.

Waivers are time-limited government programs — always confirm they're still active when you apply.

Frequently asked questions

How do I register for the Red Seal exam in Canada?

Registration is handled by your provincial apprenticeship authority. You must first complete the required hours of on-the-job training and/or your apprenticeship program, then apply through your province's trade certification body. The process varies slightly by province but generally involves submitting proof of hours, paying the exam fee, and booking a time slot at an authorized testing center.

What do I need to bring to the Red Seal exam?

You must bring: (1) valid government-issued photo ID (driver's licence or passport), (2) your exam booking confirmation or authorization number, and (3) a non-programmable calculator if permitted for your trade. No phones, smartwatches, notes, or reference materials are allowed in the exam room.

How much does the Red Seal exam cost?

Exam fees vary widely by province — from $0 to about $400 CAD. Newfoundland and Labrador eliminated all apprentice and trade-qualifier exam fees in April 2025, Ontario waives the first attempt for apprentices completing their program from 2025 onward, and Nova Scotia has waived trade-qualifier fees for construction trades until October 2026. Typical fees elsewhere: Ontario $150+HST, BC $120 (challenge application), Alberta $150, Manitoba $400 (trade qualifier exam), New Brunswick $250, Nova Scotia $350. Confirm current pricing with your provincial apprenticeship authority.

What happens if I fail the Red Seal exam?

If you fail, you can retake the exam after a waiting period that varies by province (usually 30–90 days). You will receive a result showing your performance by block/topic, which helps you identify weak areas to study. You must re-register and pay the exam fee again.

Is the Red Seal recognized in Quebec?

Quebec does not participate in the Red Seal Program. Trades in Quebec are regulated separately by the Commission de la construction du Québec (CCQ). However, a Red Seal is recognized in all other provinces and territories.
