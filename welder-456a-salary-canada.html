Red Seal Welder (456A) Salary in Canada 2026 — Province by Province

Shop welders, structural welders, pipeline welders — wages vary dramatically by industry and province. Here is the complete 2026 breakdown.

Updated September 2026

Welder wages in Canada span an enormous range — from Job Bank's national low of $22.00/hr to its high of $47.00/hr (NOC 72106, 2023–2024 wages), and higher on union mainline pipeline work, where the UA–Pipe Line Contractors Association of Canada agreement set the Western Canada welder journeyman rate at $79.49/hr from May 1, 2025. Understanding where you fall on this spectrum (and what you need to do to move up it) requires knowing how industry sector, provincial location, CWB qualifications, welding processes, and union membership each affect pay. This guide breaks down 2026 Red Seal 456A Welder wages across every major dimension.

Most pay figures on this page are Government of Canada Job Bank data for everyone working in the occupation, not only certified tradespeople. The 456A exam is 125 questions in 4 hours and the pass mark is 70%, which is 88 correct.

Province-by-Province Salary Breakdown

Alberta — $25.00–$52.18/hr (Job Bank, 2023–24 wages)

Alberta's welder median ($38.00/hr) is among the highest of any province, just behind Newfoundland and Labrador ($38.41/hr) (Job Bank, 2023–24 wages), with demand from oil sands, pipeline, and petrochemical work. Job Bank reports welder wages of $28.00–$54.00/hr (median $39.90) in the Edmonton region (2023–24 wages) and $26.16–$54.27/hr (median $37.72) in the Calgary region (2024 wages); it does not publish separate structural rates. Job Bank does not publish separate pipeline or turnaround rates; its high wage for all Alberta welders is $52.18/hr (2023–24 wages). Union mainline pipeline welders are paid more: the UA–Pipe Line Contractors Association of Canada agreement set the Western Canada welder journeyman rate at $79.49/hr from May 1, 2025. On remote sites such as oil sands turnarounds (shutdown maintenance at facilities like Syncrude, CNRL, or Suncor), camp accommodation, meals, travel, and overtime can add to total annual pay. Alberta does levy provincial income tax: 8% on the first $61,200 of taxable income for 2026 (CRA, checked September 2026).

British Columbia — $23.25–$51.87/hr (Job Bank, 2023–24 wages)

BC welder wages are highest on resource and LNG project sites. Job Bank reports welder wages of $26.00–$49.50/hr (median $37.50) in the Lower Mainland–Southwest region and $23.00–$48.00/hr (median $35.00) in Vancouver Island and Coast (2023–24 wages); it does not publish separate structural rates. Union mainline pipeline welders in BC are paid under the UA–Pipe Line Contractors Association of Canada agreement, which set the Western Canada welder journeyman rate at $72.89/hr from May 1, 2023 and $79.49/hr from May 1, 2025; Job Bank's high wage for all BC welders is $51.87/hr (2023–24 wages). LNG Canada and related Kitimat-area projects command significant site premiums. Northern BC remote project positions often offer camp accommodation; Job Bank reports welder wages of $28.74–$52.88/hr (median $37.81) in the Northeast region and $28.66–$54.60/hr (median $40.22) in the North Coast region (2024 wages). General fabrication shop welding in Metro Vancouver and aerospace welding (Boeing supply chain, Ducommun, Avcorp) for aluminum and titanium GTAW work are paid at employer-set rates, which Job Bank does not publish separately.

Ontario — $21.00–$41.28/hr (Job Bank, 2023–24 wages)

Ontario welder wages are lower than Alberta and BC due to the absence of a major oil and gas sector. Automotive supply chain welding (Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers to GM, Ford, Stellantis, Honda) is concentrated in the GTA, Hamilton, and Windsor, where Job Bank's welder medians are $28.00/hr (Toronto region), $27.00/hr (Hamilton–Niagara Peninsula) and $25.50/hr (Windsor-Sarnia) (2023–24 wages). Structural welding in the GTA on construction projects — often unionized through CLAC or building trades — pays the rates set in the applicable collective agreement, which Job Bank does not publish. Job Bank's range for all Ontario welders is $21.00–$41.28/hr, median $28.00 (2023–24 wages); it does not publish separate rates for pressure vessel (ASME-coded) work. Boilermaker work (UA Boilermakers Local 128) on refinery and chemical plant turnarounds pays competitive union rates.

Saskatchewan — $22.00–$46.00/hr (Job Bank, 2023–24 wages)

Saskatchewan welding demand is driven by potash mining, oil and gas production in the Weyburn-Estevan area, and agricultural equipment manufacturing (CNH Industrial, MacDon). Mine maintenance welders at potash and uranium operations are paid employer-set or collectively agreed rates, which Job Bank does not publish separately. For all welders, Job Bank reports $24.02–$45.15/hr (median $32.82) in the Regina–Moose Mountain region (2024 wages) and $23.00–$52.00/hr (median $32.45) in Saskatoon–Biggar (2023–24 wages); it does not publish separate construction rates. The heavy oil sector around Lloydminster (straddling the Alberta-Saskatchewan border) provides pipeline and facility maintenance welding at wages often close to Alberta's for experienced journeypersons.

Manitoba — $21.00–$40.16/hr (Job Bank, 2023–24 wages)

Manitoba welder wages are at the lower end of the national range. Job Bank's range for all welders in the Winnipeg region is $23.00–$40.16/hr, median $28.00 (2023–24 wages); it does not publish separate fabrication-shop rates. Agricultural equipment manufacturing (CNH, Versatile, New Holland) employs production welders at employer-set rates. The hydro construction sector (Manitoba Hydro, Keeyask Generating Station) has created periodic demand for structural and pipe welders at premium rates on remote northern projects.

Industry Sector Wage Comparison

Structural Steel Welding

Job Bank does not publish a separate rate for structural welders fabricating and erecting steel for buildings, bridges, and towers in Canada; its range for all welders is $22.00–$47.00/hr, median $30.00 (2023–24 wages). Union structural welders through the Ironworkers (IABSEW) or building trades earn at the higher end of this range. Structural welding work can be seasonal in cold-weather provinces, though indoor steel fabrication shops provide year-round employment. The main structural welding processes (SMAW, FCAW) are relatively straightforward to qualify in, which keeps this segment competitive and wages lower than pipeline or pressure work.

Pipeline Welding — The Wage Premium Explained

Pipeline welders are the elite earners in the Canadian welding trade, and the premium is earned:

  • 6G pipe welding is physically demanding and technically difficult — qualifying in this position takes years of focused practice
  • Remote project sites with harsh conditions (northern BC, Alberta muskeg, prairie winters)
  • High-consequence work — a failed weld on a high-pressure gas transmission line has catastrophic potential, which drives extensive NDT (non-destructive testing) inspection and zero-defect expectations
  • Extended rotations away from home (14/7, 21/7 or longer)

On union mainline pipeline projects, the UA–Pipe Line Contractors Association of Canada agreement set the Western Canada welder journeyman rate at $72.89/hr from May 1, 2023, $76.12/hr from May 1, 2024 and $79.49/hr from May 1, 2025, and pays non-local workers a subsistence allowance ($195 per calendar day from May 1, 2025; $200 from May 1, 2026) where no camp is provided. Job Bank does not publish separate pipeline rates; its high wage for all welders is $52.18/hr in Alberta and $51.87/hr in BC (2023–24 wages). Long shifts and overtime on a full-season project can raise annual earnings above a 2,000-hour estimate.

Manufacturing and Fabrication Shop Welding

Shop welding offers stable year-round employment with predictable hours but pays less than construction or pipeline work. Production GMAW (MIG) welding in automotive or general manufacturing, stainless steel TIG (GTAW) welding for food processing or pharmaceutical equipment, pressure vessel fabrication (ASME-coded) and aerospace welding (titanium, aluminum) are paid at employer-set rates that Job Bank does not publish separately; specialized process and code qualifications can command higher rates. The trade-off is workplace stability, regular hours, benefits, and no travel requirement.

Pressure Welding Premium

Welders working on pressure-rated systems (boilers, pressure vessels, steam lines, process piping) under ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code or B31 piping codes often earn a premium over structural rates (the amount varies by employer). This is because pressure welding requires specific procedure and welder qualifications (WPS/PQR under ASME IX), is subject to radiographic or ultrasonic testing, and carries significant liability. Job Bank reports a range of $37.66–$54.57/hr (median $49.00) for boilermakers in Canada and $42.35–$56.00/hr in Alberta (NOC 72103, 2023–24 wages); turnaround schedules can add shift premiums and overtime.

Union Rates for Welders in Canada

Multiple unions represent welders in Canada depending on the type of work:

  • United Association (UA) — Steamfitter / Pipefitter Division: Covers pipeline welders and pressure piping welders. UA rates are set by collective agreement. The UA also operates apprenticeship training programs considered among the best in the industry.
  • Boilermakers (International Brotherhood of Boilermakers, Iron Ship Builders): Covers industrial welders working on pressure vessels, boilers, heat exchangers, and refinery equipment. The Boilermakers have significant presence in Alberta's oil sands turnaround work. Job Bank reports $42.35–$56.00/hr for boilermakers in Alberta (NOC 72103, 2023–24 wages); union rates are set by collective agreement.
  • Ironworkers (IABSEW): Covers structural welders erecting steel on construction sites. Ironworker wages include structural ironworking work plus the welding component. Job Bank reports $32.27–$50.25/hr (median $44.34) for ironworkers in Ontario and $26.00–$50.59/hr (median $38.09) in BC (NOC 72105, 2023–24 wages); union rates are set by collective agreement.
  • Non-union: A large portion of Canadian welding work — particularly in manufacturing shops and general fabrication — is non-union. Non-union wages are set by each employer and may include benefits and profit-sharing.
CWB certification note: CWB welder qualifications (under CSA W47.1, W47.2, or W186) are process- and position-specific and expire after 2 years unless maintained through continuous work in that qualified process and position. Maintaining multiple active CWB qualifications requires regular welding practice across all qualified procedures. Some employers pay for annual requalification tests as a benefit for their certified welders.

Career Earnings Trajectory

A Red Seal Welder who follows a strategic career path can significantly increase lifetime earnings:

  1. Apprenticeship (length depends on province): pay rises each period as a percentage of the journeyperson rate — in Alberta the legal minimum is 60%, 75% and 90% over 3 periods (Alberta Tradesecrets, checked September 2026)
  2. Years 4–7 (Early Journeyperson): structural or shop work (Job Bank national welder range: $22.00–$47.00/hr, median $30.00, 2023–24) — focus on accumulating CWB qualifications, especially 6G pipe
  3. Years 7–12 (Experienced JM with Qualifications): pipeline or pressure work (union mainline pipeline welders: $79.49/hr from May 2025, UA–PLCAC agreement) — earning potential peaks as qualifications and reputation build
  4. Years 12+ (Senior/Specialty): pipeline, oil sands, or inspection roles, where pay depends on the employer and agreement; transition to CWI, welding supervisor, or welding instructor possible
