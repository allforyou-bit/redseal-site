Red Seal Exam Study Guide

Key topics, study strategies, and what to focus on — for all 8 trades

By a working Red Seal apprentice Last updated July 2026 11 trades · official exam breakdowns

How the Red Seal exam actually works

Every Red Seal exam is a multiple-choice test: 100–135 questions (depending on your trade), four options each, exactly one correct answer, and a pass mark of 70%. There are no trick essay sections and no practical component on the interprovincial written exam — but that doesn't make it easy. Questions come in three cognitive levels defined by the exam developers: Knowledge & Recall (definitions, specs, code values), Procedural & Application (how to carry out a task, in what order, with what tool), and Critical Thinking (given symptoms, find the cause — diagnosis questions).

Here's the part most candidates never look up: the mix of those question types is published for every trade, and it's dramatically different from trade to trade. A Welder exam is 40–50% pure recall; an Industrial Electrician exam is 40–50% critical thinking. That difference should decide how you study. Memorizing flashcards is a winning strategy for one trade and a losing one for another.

The 4-week study plan

Built for people working full days. About 45–60 minutes on weekdays, a bit more on weekends. It works because it front-loads finding your weaknesses instead of re-reading what you already know.

Week 1 — Baseline. Take one full mock exam cold (yes, before studying — the score doesn't matter). Then work through 20–30 practice questions per day across all topics. Let the topic progress panel show you where you actually stand. Read every explanation, even for questions you got right.
Week 2 — Topic rotation. Attack your two weakest topics from Week 1, one per session, using the topic filter. Finish each session with 10 mixed questions so strong topics don't fade. Your wrong answers are being collected automatically in the Mistakes tab — don't clear it.
Week 3 — Wrong bank + hard mode. Re-answer everything in your Mistakes tab until it's empty. Then switch the difficulty filter to hard for your strongest topics. If your trade is memorization-heavy (Welder, Ironworker), this is flashcard week: night-before Key Concept lines, every day.
Week 4 — Mock exams. Two or three full timed mocks, spaced a day or two apart, reviewing every wrong answer between attempts. Consistently above 75%? You have margin. Stuck at 65–70%? Book more time before your exam date if you can — the real thing rarely goes better than your mocks.

What to prioritize in each trade

Based on each trade's published Major Work Activity weighting — these are the blocks that decide your pass.

421A Heavy Equipment

Electrical/electronics (25 Q) and hydraulics (25 Q) are a third of your exam combined. Engines (20 Q) next. Don't skip the small blocks — HVAC, structures and hybrid together are ~16%.

310T Truck & Transport

Electrical (22 Q), engines (21 Q), steering/chassis/tires (18 Q) lead. Air brakes (17 Q) must be automatic. Trailers (8 Q) and EV (4 Q) are commonly skipped — free marks if you don't.

310S Automotive

Electrical/HVAC (23 Q) and steering/suspension/brakes/tires (23 Q) tie for the biggest blocks. Module communication (12 Q) trips up candidates from non-dealer shops — practice network diagnosis.

309A Construction Electrician

Wiring systems (30 Q) and generation/distribution/services (28 Q) are over half the exam. Know Canadian (CEC) values cold — US NEC numbers are the classic trap.

Refrigeration & AC Mechanic

Maintenance/service (28 Q) and installation (26 Q) dominate. Commissioning (21 Q) is bigger than most people expect — startup, charging and verification procedures.

Welder

Welding processes are 54 of 125 questions. Layout & fabrication (28 Q) next. With 40–50% recall questions, flashcards on symbols, gases, electrodes and codes pay off more here than in any other trade.

Plumber

Drainage/DWV (32 Q) and water supply (24 Q) are the core. Low-pressure steam & hot water heating (16 Q) is the block apprentices see least on the job — study it deliberately.

Industrial Electrician

With 40–50% critical-thinking questions, symptom-to-cause practice beats re-reading theory. Motors, controls, PLCs and instrumentation scenarios are where the exam is won.

Ironworker

The most memorization-heavy Red Seal exam (50–60% recall). Rigging capacities, hand signals, bolt specs, WHMIS — drill facts daily. Critical thinking is under 10%.

Gas Fitter

Code-procedure heavy: appliance installation, venting and piping per CSA B149.1. Combustion analysis and pressure testing procedures come up constantly.

Sheet Metal Worker

Procedural exam (60–70%): pattern development and layout steps, fabrication sequences, seam/joint selection. Practice the how-to order, not just definitions.

Three study techniques that match how the exam is written

1. Active recall over re-reading

Answering questions (and getting some wrong) builds far more durable memory than re-reading notes. That's the whole reason this site is a question bank and not a textbook. Struggle first, then read the explanation.

2. Spaced repetition on your weak spots

Revisit your Mistakes tab on day 1, day 3, and day 7 after collecting an error. Each successful recall at a longer interval roughly doubles how long the fact sticks.

3. Train the question format itself

Red Seal questions reward careful reading: watch for "MOST likely", "FIRST", and "EXCEPT"; eliminate one obviously wrong option before deciding; and treat any suspiciously US-flavoured number (NEC/OSHA values) as a trap in Canadian code questions. Never leave a blank — there's no penalty for guessing.
