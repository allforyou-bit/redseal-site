EI for apprentices in technical training: who may qualify, and up to $729 a week

Who can get EI regular benefits during full-time technical training, the 16-digit reference code, when to apply, the one waiting week, and an estimate for your training block.

Updated November 2026 EI pages checked November 5, 2026
Checked on November 5, 2026 against the Government of Canada’s Employment Insurance for apprentices page and its EI regular benefits pages on the amount and who qualifies. This page is an independent guide, not a government site. Service Canada decides whether you qualify and how much you receive; apply only through the official service. The $400 Apprenticeship Training Grant announced for 2027 is not in effect yet.

Estimate your EI for a training block

The estimate uses the basic rate that applies to most people (55%), the $729 weekly maximum from January 1, 2026, and leaves out the 1-week waiting period when it applies. It rounds to the nearest dollar, so treat it as approximate, and it is before tax. It does not check whether you qualify or have enough insurable hours. The announced Apprenticeship Training Grant is shown on a separate line and is not added in, because it is not in effect and how to apply has not been published.

Who can get EI during technical training

If your province or territory has referred you to full-time technical training (sometimes called block-release training) as part of your apprenticeship, you may be able to receive EI regular benefits while you attend the course. You need a referral for each block of full-time technical training.

Other EI benefits may also apply: sickness, maternity, parental or caregiving benefits.

The 16-digit reference code

The reference code is your proof of referral. Your province, territory or training institution issues it once you are approved to attend full-time technical training, and a new code is issued for every block.

  • When you apply: give the code on your application to avoid delays.
  • No code yet, or you do not know it: contact your province, territory or training institution.
  • Applied without it, or already on EI and about to start training: contact Service Canada with the code as soon as you can.

How to apply

  1. Have the 16-digit reference code for this block ready; your province, territory or training institution issues it. Giving it when you apply avoids delays. If you apply without it, contact Service Canada with the code as soon as you can.
  2. Apply for EI online. You can apply as early as 7 days before your last day of work.
  3. Check the status of your claim, and your electronic records of employment, in My Service Canada Account.

The waiting week is served once per program

The 1-week waiting period is served only once for the same apprenticeship program. If you served it at an earlier block of the same program, it does not apply again at a later block.

Reports every 2 weeks

EI regular benefits normally require a report every 2 weeks to receive payments. Apprentices can ask to be exempt from these biweekly reports. If you asked for the exemption and then work or earn money, contact Service Canada to report it.

When benefits end, and if you lose your job

If you are on leave from work to attend full-time technical training, your EI benefits end on the last day of the course.

If you lose your job through no fault of your own before the end date of your claim, you may be able to keep receiving regular benefits while you look for work. In general, EI regular benefits last 14 to 45 weeks, depending on your region’s unemployment rate and your insurable hours.

How the weekly amount is worked out

For most people the basic rate is 55% of average insurable weekly earnings, up to a maximum. The average comes from your best weeks: your total insurable earnings over the best 14 to 22 weeks, depending on your region’s unemployment rate (14 in the regions with the highest unemployment, 22 in those with the lowest), divided by that number of weeks.

As of January 1, 2026 the maximum yearly insurable earnings is $68,900, which puts the maximum at $729 a week. Taxes are deducted from EI payments.

Hours you need to qualify

EI regular benefits need 420 to 700 hours of insurable employment in your qualifying period, depending on the unemployment rate in your region: 700 hours where it is 6% or less, and 420 where it is above 13%. You need more if you have received a notice of violation. Look up your EI economic region by postal code to find the hours you need, starting from the Do you qualify page.

The qualifying period is the shorter of the 52 weeks before your claim starts and the time since the start of a previous benefit period approved in the last 52 weeks. In some cases it can be extended to 104 weeks.

Announced: $400 a week for training weeks from January 3, 2027

Announced, not in effect yet on November 5, 2026. ESDC’s Team Canada Strong backgrounder of October 23, 2026 announces an Apprenticeship Training Grant: a $400 weekly top-up while apprentices attend mandatory in-class technical training, paid in addition to EI benefits, for eligible weeks of technical training beginning on or after January 3, 2027. The backgrounder does not say how to apply or give a total amount.

The other supports in the same backgrounder are summarised on the Red Seal Completion Bonus page.

Other support for apprentices

The Government of Canada’s apprentice page also points to the Canada Apprentice Loan and to tax deductions and credits for apprentices and tradespersons, and says provinces and territories may help with accommodation, childcare and transportation costs.

  • The Canada Apprentice Loan lends up to $4,000 per period of technical training, interest-free.
  • Employed tradespeople who have to provide their own tools can look at the tradesperson’s tools deduction.
  • Journeypersons who completed an apprenticeship and were certified in a designated Red Seal trade on or after April 1, 2025 can look at the $5,000 Red Seal Completion Bonus.

Frequently asked questions

Can apprentices get EI during technical training?

Apprentices may be able to receive EI regular benefits while they attend full-time technical training, sometimes called block-release training, that their province or territory has referred them to as part of their apprenticeship. A referral is needed for each block, and you can apply online as early as 7 days before your last day of work.

What is the 16-digit reference code for EI apprentices?

It is the proof of your referral to full-time technical training. Your province, territory or training institution issues it once you are approved to attend, with a new code for each block. Give it when you apply to avoid delays; if you do not know it or have not received it, contact your province, territory or training institution. If you applied without it, or you are already on EI and about to start training, contact Service Canada with the code as soon as possible.

Do apprentices serve the EI waiting week for every training block?

No. The 1-week waiting period is served only once for the same apprenticeship program, so it does not apply again at later blocks of that program.

How much EI do apprentices get during technical training?

For most people, 55% of average insurable weekly earnings, up to a maximum of $729 a week as of January 1, 2026, when the maximum yearly insurable earnings is $68,900. The average uses your best 14 to 22 weeks, depending on your region's unemployment rate, and taxes are deducted from EI payments.

When does EI end for an apprentice in technical training?

If you are on leave from work to attend full-time technical training, EI benefits end on the last day of the course. If you lose your job through no fault of your own before the end date of your claim, you may be able to continue receiving regular benefits while you look for work.

What is the $400 a week Apprenticeship Training Grant?

An announced weekly top-up of $400 while apprentices attend mandatory in-class technical training, paid in addition to EI benefits, for eligible weeks of technical training beginning on or after January 3, 2027. It was not in effect on November 5, 2026, and ESDC's October 23, 2026 backgrounder does not say how to apply or give a total amount.
