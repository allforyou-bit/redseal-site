Labour mobility deduction: up to $4,000 a year for working away from home

For tradespeople and apprentices in construction who take a temporary job far from home: the 150 km and 36-hour tests, what counts, the limits, and a calculator.

Tax year 2025 Checked against canada.ca, November 4, 2026
These are the rules for the 2025 tax year, from Chapter 7 of the Canada Revenue Agency’s Guide T4044, Employment Expenses 2025, read on November 4, 2026. This page is an independent guide, not tax advice and not a government site: CRA’s guide and your own tax professional are the authority.

The short answer

WhoTradespeople and apprentices with employment income whose work is construction activities.
The distance testYour temporary lodging is at least 150 km closer to each temporary work location than your home, and you are required to be away from home for at least 36 hours.
How muchUp to $4,000 of eligible expenses a year. For each relocation, also no more than 50% of your employment income that year from construction work at that location.
What countsTemporary lodging (if your home stays your principal residence, available for you to live in, and is not rented out), one round trip home to lodging, and the meals on that trip.
Not countedGetting around at the work location; anything you are entitled to have reimbursed or that is covered by an allowance or other assistance; and anything already deducted elsewhere, such as moving expenses.
Where it goesCalculate it on page 2 of Form T777, enter it on page 1 line 11, and the total goes on line 22900 of your return.
Left overUnused expenses carry forward to the next year against income from the same relocation.

Check the tests and work out the deduction

The calculator follows the rules in Guide T4044 for one relocation. The $4,000 limit is for the whole year, so with several relocations in a year the total of all of them cannot pass $4,000. It does not check the other conditions, such as a temporary employment contract or the work location being outside the locality where you ordinarily work. The second example’s result ($2,250) is worked out on this page from the limits; CRA’s guide shows it in a table. The first example combines CRA’s December 2025 expenses with the distances from its separate Toronto to Montreal example.

Who qualifies

CRA’s eligible tradesperson is a tradesperson or an apprentice who has employment income and whose employment duties are construction activities. Construction activities cover the erection, excavation, installation, alteration, modification, repair, improvement, demolition, destruction, dismantling or removal of all or part of a building, structure, surface or sub-surface construction, or similar property. Only employment income counts: the 50% limit uses your employment income from construction activities at the relocation’s work location.

The relocation tests

The relocation has to be temporary and meet all of these:

  • you relocate to work at one or more temporary work locations in the same locality, under a temporary employment contract;
  • the work location is in Canada and outside the locality, such as a city, where you ordinarily work or carry on a business;
  • before the relocation you ordinarily lived at a home in Canada, and you had to be away from it for at least 36 hours;
  • you took up temporary lodging in Canada that is at least 150 km closer to each temporary work location than your home. With two lodgings in one relocation, such as two hotels, each one has to pass this test.

CRA’s example: you live and work in Toronto and take a 3-week contract on a construction site in Montreal, 550 km away, renting a short-term apartment 10 km from the site. Home is more than 150 km further from the site than the apartment, so the relocation qualifies.

What counts, and when

  • Temporary lodging, but only if your home stays your principal residence, available for you to live in, and is not rented to anyone else. If you sublet your home, only the round trip and its meals count.
  • Transportation for one round trip between home and the temporary lodging.
  • Meals you eat during that round trip.

The expenses must be reasonable and can be incurred in the previous year, the current year or before February 1 of the next year. They cannot have been deducted before, deducted in another way such as moving expenses, or be something you are entitled to have reimbursed, or that is paid by an allowance or other assistance.

The limits, and carrying expenses forward

The deduction is at most $4,000 a year. For each relocation it is also limited to 50% of your employment income that year from construction activities at that relocation’s work location, counting any expenses carried forward. Each relocation is worked out separately, so you can have more than one in a year. Whatever you cannot deduct, because the income from that relocation was too low or the $4,000 was reached, carries forward to the next year against income from the same relocation.

CRA’s example: a relocation starts on December 28, 2025, with a $1,000 round-trip flight, $100 of meals on the way and $400 of lodging in December. No pay from that job arrives until January 2026, so nothing is deductible for 2025 and the $1,500 carries forward. In 2026 the job pays $7,000, and $650 of lodging and $100 of meals on the flight home are added, for $2,250 in all. CRA’s guide shows the 2026 result in a table; applying the limits above gives the lesser of $2,250, $4,000 and half of $7,000, which is $2,250 (worked out on this page).

Other support for tradespeople and apprentices

Employed tradespeople who buy their own tools can also look at the tradesperson’s tools deduction. Apprentices in technical training can borrow through the Canada Apprentice Loan, and journeypersons who completed a registered apprenticeship and were certified on or after April 1, 2025 can look at the $5,000 Red Seal Completion Bonus.

Frequently asked questions

What is the labour mobility deduction for tradespeople?

A deduction from employment income for tradespeople and apprentices in construction who temporarily relocate to work at a site far from home. It covers temporary lodging, one round trip and the meals on that trip, up to $4,000 a year. You calculate it on Form T777 and claim it on line 22900.

How far from home does the job have to be for the labour mobility deduction?

Among other conditions, your temporary lodging has to be at least 150 kilometres closer to each temporary work location than your ordinary residence, you have to be required to be away from home for at least 36 hours, and the work location has to be outside the locality where you ordinarily work or carry on a business.

How much can I claim under the labour mobility deduction?

Up to $4,000 of eligible temporary relocation expenses a year. For each relocation the claim, including any amount carried forward, is also limited to 50% of your employment income that year from construction activities at that relocation's work location.

Which expenses count for the labour mobility deduction?

Temporary lodging, as long as your home stays your principal residence, available to you and not rented out; transportation for one round trip between home and the temporary lodging; and the meals you eat during that round trip. Getting around at the work location, such as a rental car there, does not count, and neither does anything you are entitled to have reimbursed or that is covered by an allowance or other assistance.

Can apprentices claim the labour mobility deduction?

Yes. CRA defines an eligible tradesperson as a tradesperson or an apprentice who has employment income and performs employment duties in construction activities.

What if my expenses are more than I can deduct this year?

The unused part carries forward to the following year and can be deducted against employment income from the same relocation, for example when the work and the pay continue into January.
